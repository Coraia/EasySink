The reasoning of these two men did not carry conviction, since it was felt that the Absolute or Inﬁnite does not necessarily exist outside of all relations, but can enter into various relations; and that the fact that we know things only in their relations does not mean that the knowledge so acquired is merely a relative or unreal knowledge.

An account of reality, which excludes every relation, may protect an abstract definition while leaving ordinary knowledge unexplained. The question is not whether relations exist, but whether they exhaust what a thing is.

To understand the argument, we must distinguish the way in which knowledge is acquired from the claim that such knowledge is unreal. A relation can be part of our understanding without being the whole of what we understand.
