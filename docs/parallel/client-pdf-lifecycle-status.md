## Failure inventory before experiment code

- A converter leader exits first: `terminate` returns on its returncode, leaving live grandchildren, including those with closed/inherited pipes. Waiting only for the leader can miss SIGTERM-resistant group members.
- A child holds inherited stdout/stderr after the leader exits: reading or `wait()` can hang; shutdown must escalate the owned group and drain/reap the direct child.
- Backend dies between persisting cancelled/paused and clearing PID: startup must clean verified owned workers without reviving cancellation or adopting its late results.
- PID reuse/unrelated process, relocated application path, stale command file or absent leader: never signal an unverified process. Recovery must retain enough ownership evidence and report cases it cannot establish.
- Capability probes race close or timeout, with SSH descendants: they must not outlive completed shutdown or leave children after successful probe output.
- Async task cancellation during spawn/publication, disk write or malformed worker output: finally paths must clean owned subprocesses, retain complete checkpoints and not report false completion.
- Queued, paused, cancelled, unknown-engine tasks after restart: no unsolicited dispatch or changed range/service; completed material remains readable. Paid result recovery remains outside this local-only implementation.
- Bundle has no `.venv-pdf-lab`: fallback must be its running `sys.executable`, with digital dependencies present and resources found after relocation, without repository or shell-development dependencies.
- Upload validators, static assets, model/lexicon paths outside ownership may still assume source checkout layout; report concrete integration requirements to main, do not edit them.
- Public original/result/asset routes must retain registered-file boundaries and range limits; internal ownership metadata must not be added to public task payloads.

First failures and hypotheses will be recorded before production edits; no blanket #12/#14 or V1 acceptance is implied.

- Initial finding: `pdf/uploads.py` had no cancellation/finally cleanup for its validation subprocess (only timeout cleanup). Relocating an app while an orphan from its old path survives needs retained launch identity; do not relax verification to kill a stale PID. A leader that died before restart, leaving only unidentifiable descendants, cannot safely be signalled by a bare stored PID. These are real recovery limits to report until verified ownership is available.

## Delivered changes

- `worker.py`: terminate the entire owned process group, including after its leader exits; SIGCONT/SIGTERM, up to five seconds of grace, SIGKILL and up to two seconds of group disappearance observation. Await/reap the direct subprocess; grandchildren are reparented/reaped by the OS. One shielded cleanup per Process avoids competing close/run signals. A small leader-exit watcher prevents inherited child pipes keeping a dead converter's job alive. Capabilities' subprocesses participate in shutdown and cleanup after success. Recovery now also cleans a verified PID left on a cancelled record, without dispatching or adopting its late result.
- `local_environment`: explicitly forward `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE`, `PYTHONNOUSERSITE`, `XDG_CACHE_HOME`, `PDF_LAB_MODELS_DIR`, `HF_HOME`, `HF_HUB_CACHE`, `HUGGINGFACE_HUB_CACHE`, `TRANSFORMERS_CACHE`, `TORCH_HOME`. Preserve offline/telemetry/thread limits and credential filtering. The native runtime supplies writable data/cache/model paths; cwd remains resource ROOT. Confirmed actual `conversion.model_directory()` reads **PDF_LAB_MODELS_DIR**, exactly matching the native backend. This does not make optional OCR ready or bypass its admission checks.
- `uploads.py`: minimal `finally` cleanup around actual validator communication using the same owned-worker helper; receive/validate/register behavior unchanged.
- No changes to conversion.py, module.py, DB schema, readiness.py or API paths by this sidecar. Before snapshots of worker/readiness match main's original baseline byte-for-byte; worker diff is relative to that saved dirty version, not HEAD.
